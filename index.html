Pack your bags.
You’re going somewhere.

Today you won’t study a trip. You’ll plan one, land in it, chat with strangers, and deal with whatever goes wrong. English only, no scripts.

Boarding pass
  • Choose where you’re going
  • Learn what people actually say
  • Live four scenes on the ground
  • Tell a friend how it went
Part 1 · Travel dreaming · 5 min

Where would you go?

Pick any place on the list, or type your own. It’s your trip.

If you could travel anywhere right now, where would you go?
What draws you to this place?
What would your ideal trip look like?
Would you explore like a local or hit the main sights?
Who would you travel with, if anyone?
What unexpected experience would make the trip unforgettable?
Teacher: Don’t pick for her. Open with: “Okay, forget your schedule for a second. If a ticket landed in your lap tonight, where are you headed?” Then follow her answer: “Ooh, what’s the first thing you picture when you think of it?” Note her destination, travel style and companion; you’ll reuse them in Parts 2 and 4.
Part 2 · Build your travel experience · 8 min

You’ve just landed in your destination.

Make decisions out loud and explain them. There are no right answers.

Where are you staying?

Follow up: What made you choose that over a central hotel? Would you rather have a view or a good location?

How do you get around?

Follow up: Would you ever rent a bike or just walk until your feet give up? What if it rains?

You’ve dropped your bags and the afternoon is empty. What are you in the mood for?

Follow up: Would you plan something in advance, or wander and see where the day takes you?

How would you find a hidden gem?

Follow up: Who would you ask? What would you look for on a menu or a street?

Someone offers you a local dish you’ve never heard of.

Follow up: Do you say yes straight away? What would you ask first?

Your plans change at the last minute. A museum is closed, a train is cancelled.

Follow up: Are you the type to panic or improvise? What did you do last time?

Teacher: This is a conversation, not a quiz. Skip questions she’s already answered. React first (“No way, I’d have done the same!”), then ask. Jot down 2–3 language slips to revisit in the feedback, and don’t interrupt.
Part 3 · 15 min

Real-life English: what people actually say

Chunks, softeners and phrases that make you sound natural. Pick the ones you’d really use. Orange text is a more casual option.

ExpressionWhen to use itIn real life

Roleplay 1: At the pharmacy

Your goal: You’ve got a headache and a sore throat on day two of your trip. Find something that helps and check it’s safe.

Teacher (pharmacist): “Hi there, what can I help you with today?”

Follow-ups: “Have you tried anything for it yet?” · “Any allergies I should know about?” · “Do you get drowsy on that one? I’m driving later.” (Now it’s her turn to ask questions.)

Roleplay 2: In the queue, then at the café

Your goal: The line is huge. Start a chat with the person next to you, then order something with a small change and fix the mistake when it arrives.

Teacher (stranger in line): “Ugh, this is taking forever. Do you know if this is the line for the pastries too?”

Follow-ups: “Is this your first time here?” · “Oh, you’re from Brazil? What brings you here?” · Barista: “Sorry, was that oat milk or regular?” (It was the wrong one.)

Teacher: Show a few expressions, then play. Never give a model answer. If she freezes, offer a first word, not a full sentence. Let her pick which tab to practise; don’t drill all six.
Part 4 · Immersive roleplay · 12 min

You’re in your destination now!

Four scenes, one day. Scenes unfold as you go, so you won’t know what’s coming. Keep conversations alive: ask, react, clarify, change the subject.

Tools for real conversations

Show interest
“No way! How did that happen?”
Ask a follow-up
“And what did you do after that?”
Clarify
“Sorry, did you say left at the church?”
Keep it going
“That reminds me, have you been to…?”
Change the subject
“Anyway, what’s good to eat around here?”
Part 5 · Reflection & speaking challenge · 5 min

Tell a friend how your day went.

Talk freely, without notes. Make it sound like you’re on a call with your best friend that night.

Where you went
What you did
Someone you met
The unexpected moment
What you’d do differently next time
What was the most memorable part of your day, and what made it so special?
Feedback (about 1 minute, communication first): Fluency and spontaneity · vocabulary range · natural expressions she used · how well she kept conversations going. Share two strengths and one or two things to try next time, plus 2–3 phrases from your notes, rephrased more naturally. Don’t correct as she speaks.
Fluency
Did she keep going when she got stuck?
Range
Did she use new chunks, not just safe words?
Naturalness
Softeners, reactions, fillers?
Interaction
Did she ask, react and clarify?
Safe travels. Keep talking.
